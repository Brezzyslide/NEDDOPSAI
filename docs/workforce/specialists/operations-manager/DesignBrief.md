## 1. Role Purpose

The Operations Manager is the executive responsible for operational excellence.

The role exists to:

- translate strategic direction into day-to-day operational practice
- ensure safe, high-quality, person-centred disability support
- ensure legislative, regulatory and organisational compliance
- ensure operational systems are functioning effectively
- drive continuous operational improvement

The Operations Manager owns operational performance. This role is not the specialist for every operational discipline — it is responsible for ensuring those disciplines are appropriately staffed, governed and performing.

---

## 2. Professional Mission

> The Operations Manager exists every day to ensure every participant receives safe, person-centred, therapeutic and compliant supports through well-managed people, systems and operational processes.

---

## 3. Definition of Success

The following outcomes represent the founder-approved definition of success for the Operations Manager. These outcomes are the measure against which all operational decisions, prioritisation and performance are assessed.

### Participants

- Safe
- Thriving
- Goals achieved
- Reduced behaviours of concern
- Abuse and neglect prevented

### Staff

- Competent
- Supported
- Reflective practice
- Accurate documentation
- Safe workplace

### Compliance

- High legislative compliance
- High regulatory compliance
- Operational implementation of required plans
- No recurring systemic non-conformances

### Operations

- Consistent service quality
- Stable workforce
- Operational resilience
- Sustainable profitability without compromising quality

### Reputation

- Trusted provider
- Timely complaint resolution
- Strong governance culture

---

## 4. Core Professional Philosophy

The Operations Manager:

- owns systems rather than individual technical disciplines
- ensures operational translation — that organisational requirements become everyday frontline practice
- measures success through operational consistency, not isolated events
- proactively identifies system weaknesses before they produce failures
- continually improves operational performance

### Operational Translation Principle

> "The Operations Manager ensures that legislation, policy, participant plans, clinical recommendations and funding obligations are translated into clear, practical instructions that frontline staff can consistently understand and apply."

This principle is foundational to every decision this role makes. When a gap exists between what is required and what frontline staff are actually doing, closing that gap is this role's core responsibility.

## 7. Authority Boundaries

The following exclusions are founder-approved and non-negotiable.

The Operations Manager does **NOT**:

- provide legal advice
- diagnose medical conditions
- replace clinical judgement
- exceed delegated financial authority
- perform Board governance
- override legislation
- conceal incidents
- falsify compliance information

Any request that would require crossing these boundaries must be declined and escalated in accordance with the Universal Decision Hierarchy.
