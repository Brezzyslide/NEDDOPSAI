SPRINT 29K.4 — CLAIM INTEGRITY HARDENING & ABSENCE VERIFICATION

This sprint follows Sprint 29K.3.

Sprint 29K.3 established:
- claims are emitted during the original specialist generation
- no second LLM reconstruction pass
- no second KRS retrieval for ordinary positive claim binding
- claims are version-scoped
- observations require evidence bindings
- supporting spans must exist verbatim in the referenced EvidencePack chunk
- contradictions require both sides
- recommendations require related parent claims
- external requirements cannot be grounded from model training knowledge
- absence findings are currently honestly stored as unverified_absence
- provenance failures are durably recorded
- evidence/claim metadata does not leak into the human-readable Completed Work
- tenant isolation is enforced

DO NOT redesign or replace these mechanisms.

The purpose of Sprint 29K.4 is to close THREE remaining integrity risks:

1. SEMANTIC SUPPORT RISK
A real passage may exist and contain the quoted words, while not actually supporting the claim made from it.

2. CLAIM-TYPE RISK
The specialist may incorrectly label an inference as an observation, allowing professional interpretation to appear as documentary fact.

3. ABSENCE-PROOF RISK
“Nothing was retrieved” does not prove “the requirement is absent.”

This is an integrity sprint, not an Evidence UI sprint.

Do not build the Evidence UI yet.

========================================================
PART B — DEFINE THE CLAIM INTEGRITY CONTRACT
========================================================

Implement a server-enforced integrity contract.

The specialist does NOT get to decide by itself that a claim is grounded.

Claim type determines the validation rule.

Required conceptual rules:

OBSERVATION
- must reference EvidencePack evidence
- referenced chunk must belong to the execution EvidencePack
- source/version must match
- supporting span must exist in the referenced full chunk
- evidence must substantively support the claim
- material factual elements must not contradict the evidence

ABSENCE_FINDING
- must go through the targeted absence-verification process defined below
- cannot become verified_absence merely because ordinary retrieval returned no result

INFERENCE
- must be explicitly stored as claimType=inference
- must have one or more valid grounded/verified parent claims
- cannot masquerade as direct documentary observation
- should use appropriately qualified language where appropriate
- evidence chain must remain visible through relatedClaimIds

RECOMMENDATION
- must have valid parent finding(s)
- cannot be independently represented as a documentary fact
- parent claims must exist in the same Completed Work version / tenant

EXTERNAL_REQUIREMENT
- must bind to an approved external-authority source
- model training knowledge is never sufficient
- if no trusted external source exists, status remains unsupported_external or equivalent

CONTRADICTION
- requires at least two competing evidence passages
- both passages must be valid
- the contradiction must concern the same material proposition
- one passage alone can never establish a contradiction

========================================================
PART C — SEMANTIC SUPPORT VALIDATION
========================================================

Current exact-span verification answers:

“Do these words exist in this chunk?”

It does NOT answer:

“Does this passage actually support this claim?”

Close that gap.

Example adversarial case:

Evidence:
“The Complaints Officer must acknowledge complaints within five business days.”

Claim:
“All complaints must be resolved within five business days.”

A span containing “complaints” and “five business days” is real, but the claim is not supported.

Design a layered validation mechanism.

At minimum distinguish:

1. passage_verified
   The claimed supporting span genuinely exists in the referenced full chunk.

2. semantic_support
   Whether the evidence actually supports the material proposition.

3. material_fact_consistency
   Actors, actions, quantities, dates, timeframes, obligations, negation and other material facts must not conflict.

Do not silently treat semantic similarity as proof.

Do not use a second unrestricted LLM to rewrite or reconstruct the approved claim.

If a semantic model/judge is used, its judgement must be treated as a validation signal, not infallible truth.

Investigate whether deterministic checks can first detect:
- numbers
- timeframes
- named actors/roles
- modal obligation terms such as must/should/may
- negation
- action verbs
- materially different subjects/objects

Then determine whether a bounded semantic entailment check is needed.

Required outcome:

A passage can be:
- verified and supporting
- verified but support_uncertain
- verified but contradictory
- invalid/not present

A claim must never become grounded merely because its quotation exists.

========================================================
PART D — CLAIM-TYPE INTEGRITY
========================================================

The server must not blindly trust claimType emitted by the specialist.

Investigate practical validation rules that distinguish documentary observations from interpretation.

Test at least:

Evidence:
“The policy does not identify an escalation decision-maker.”

Claim A:
“The policy does not identify an escalation decision-maker.”
Expected: absence_finding, NOT ordinary observation unless directly expressed by an authoritative passage.

Claim B:
“Lack of escalation ownership may lead to inconsistent complaint handling.”
Expected: inference.

Claim C:
“Lack of escalation ownership causes inconsistent complaint handling.”
Expected: inference and potentially support_uncertain because causation is stronger than the evidence.

Claim D:
“The organisation handles high-severity complaints inconsistently.”
Expected: unsupported observation unless evidence directly demonstrates this.

The validator should detect obvious type-rule violations.

Do NOT claim perfect semantic classification.

Where type cannot safely be established, downgrade the claim rather than accepting the model's preferred type.

Report what remains model-dependent.

========================================================
PART E — TARGETED ABSENCE VERIFICATION
========================================================

This is the central requirement of Sprint 29K.4.

Current unverified_absence behaviour is correct and must remain the default until verification succeeds.

For each absence claim, perform a TARGETED evidence search specifically for that claim.

This is an intentional additional KRS retrieval ONLY for evidence-required absence claims.

It must NOT cause ordinary positive claims to re-retrieve evidence.

Example claim:

“The Complaints Management Policy does not define an escalation timeframe.”

A single literal search for “escalation timeframe” is NOT sufficient.

Generate/use a bounded concept search family such as:

- escalation timeframe
- escalation time limit
- escalation deadline
- escalation period
- referral timeframe
- management escalation
- escalation procedure
- high severity escalation

Do not permit uncontrolled query explosion.

Persist exactly what queries were executed.

========================================================
PART F — ABSENCE SEARCH UNIVERSE
========================================================

Before absence can be verified, establish the universe being searched.

Persist:

- organisationId
- sourceId(s)
- sourceVersionId(s)
- source title/version display reference
- search scope mode
- specialistCode where applicable
- whether each source/version is fully ingested/searchable
- retrieval method
- active threshold/configuration
- search terms executed
- number of candidates examined
- relevant sections examined
- top relevance scores
- any matching/contradictory passages found

A claim MUST NOT be broader than its search universe.

Example:

VALID:
“No escalation timeframe was found in Complaints Management Policy v1.0.”

INVALID unless separately proven:
“The organisation has no escalation timeframe.”

The first is document-scoped.
The second is organisation-scoped.

Implement scope validation.

Persist a human-readable scope label, not merely UUIDs.

========================================================
PART G — ABSENCE OUTCOME MODEL
========================================================

Do not use binary found/not-found logic.

Implement at least these outcomes:

verified_absence
- source/version scope is known
- required sources are available
- ingestion/search coverage is sufficient
- bounded targeted searches completed
- no matching requirement found
- no contradictory evidence found
- scope of final claim does not exceed search scope

unverified_absence
- no requirement found BUT coverage/search quality is insufficient
- source unavailable/incomplete
- retrieval failure
- weak search coverage
- ambiguous result
- any other condition preventing strong absence proof

contradicted_absence
- targeted search finds evidence showing that the supposedly absent requirement actually exists

If contradicted_absence occurs:
- the absence claim must NOT be represented as grounded
- retain the evidence that contradicted it
- make the failure auditable

Never turn retrieval failure into verified absence.

========================================================
PART I — CONFIDENCE RULE
========================================================

Confidence must never manufacture certainty.

A high model confidence score must not override:
- missing evidence
- incomplete ingestion
- insufficient search coverage
- contradictory evidence
- source/version integrity failure
- tenant mismatch

Define the confidence calculation transparently.

If confidenceOfAbsence cannot be defensibly derived from measurable retrieval/coverage signals, store null rather than inventing a number.

========================================================
PART J — EVIDENCE-REQUIRED TASK GATE
========================================================

Investigate and design the gate now.

We do NOT want every task using this machinery.

Examples normally requiring evidence integrity:
- policy review
- compliance review
- audit
- investigation
- due diligence
- regulatory comparison
- evidence-based analysis
- formal risk assessment
- document gap analysis
- work explicitly requiring citations or source-grounded findings

Examples normally NOT requiring claim provenance:
- ordinary email
- rewrite
- tone improvement
- meeting note
- simple internal message
- brainstorming
- generic drafting with no source-grounding requirement
- routine administrative communication

Do not rely only on keyword detection.

First inspect whether task manifests, capability definitions, work packages, specialist configuration, resource plans or existing execution metadata already provide a suitable deterministic signal.

Preferred architecture:

evidenceMode:
  "required"
  | "optional"
  | "none"

or equivalent existing architecture.

Rules:
- required → evidence retrieval + claim provenance + absence verification where applicable
- optional → evidence may be used when supplied/relevant but do not force unnecessary provenance
- none → ordinary generation path; no claim integrity overhead

Do not implement a parallel task-classification architecture if an existing task/capability contract can carry this flag.

For this sprint, implement the gate only if it can be added safely without destabilising execution. Otherwise produce the exact implementation design and make it the immediate follow-on gate.

========================================================
PART L — ADVERSARIAL TESTS
========================================================

Add tests specifically designed to fool shallow evidence checking.

At minimum:

L1 — Same words, different meaning
Passage contains same nouns/numbers but does not support proposition.

L2 — Wrong timeframe
Passage says 5 days; claim says 3.

L3 — Wrong actor
Passage assigns Service Manager; claim assigns Complaints Officer.

L4 — Must vs may
Passage says “may”; claim says “must”.

L5 — Negation reversal
Passage says requirement does NOT apply; claim says it applies.

L6 — Inference masquerading as observation.

L7 — Causal inference stronger than evidence.

L8 — Absence search with incomplete source ingestion.
Must remain unverified_absence.

L9 — Absence search finds synonym wording.
Must become contradicted_absence, not verified_absence.

L10 — Absence search finds requirement in appendix.
Must become contradicted_absence.

L11 — Organisation-wide absence claim based on one document.
Reject/downgrade due to scope mismatch.

L12 — Retrieval service failure.
Must remain unverified_absence.

L13 — Cross-tenant source supplied during absence verification.
Reject before persistence.

L14 — External requirement based only on model knowledge.
unsupported_external.

L15 — Recommendation whose parent claim is invalid.
Recommendation cannot become grounded.

from

“What the system has sufficient evidence to represent as true.”

Uncertainty must DOWNGRADE a claim.

Uncertainty must never be converted into certainty merely because the model sounds confident.
