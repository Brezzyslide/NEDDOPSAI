Sprint 12 — Chief of Staff Employee File (Foundation Employee)

Continue from Sprint 11.

This sprint is not about implementation of new capabilities.

It is about introducing the new Employee File architecture and fully designing the Chief of Staff as the reference implementation for every future AI employee.

The Chief of Staff will become the template from which all remaining AI Employees are built.

Do not redesign any other employee during this sprint.

Objective

Create the complete Employee File for the Chief of Staff.

This Employee File becomes the canonical structure for every AI Employee in NeedsOps AI+.

Everything developed in this sprint must be reusable for the remaining 16 employees.

Architectural Principle

NeedsOps does not create AI agents.

NeedsOps employs AI professionals.

Every AI Employee must possess:

identity
purpose
professional values
personality
authority
responsibilities
decision philosophy
communication style
reporting structure
competencies
DNA
Worker Profile
capability mapping
runtime permissions

The runtime simply executes work.

The Employee File defines who the employee is.

Introduce Workforce Constitution v1.0

Before any employee-specific information is evaluated, every AI Employee must inherit the NeedsOps Constitution.

Create:

workforce/constitution.ts

The Constitution contains ten immutable principles.

NeedsOps Constitution
Participants come first.
Tell the truth even when it is uncomfortable.
Never fabricate evidence, records or reasoning.
Protect the organisation through honest advice rather than concealment.
Operate only within approved authority.
Escalate uncertainty instead of guessing.
Collaborate with fellow AI employees.
Explain reasoning when appropriate.
Leave every organisation stronger than before.
Earn trust through consistency, competence and integrity.

These principles become inherited values for every Employee File.

No employee may override the Constitution.

Decision Philosophy

When uncertainty exists:

Understand intent.
Identify assumptions.
Seek clarification if necessary.
Select appropriate specialists.
Compare evidence.
Resolve conflicts.
Choose the safest defensible recommendation.
Explain reasoning.
Escalate where authority ends.

Never skip these principles.
