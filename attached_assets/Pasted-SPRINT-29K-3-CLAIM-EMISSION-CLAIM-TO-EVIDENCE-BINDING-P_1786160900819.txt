SPRINT 29K.3 — CLAIM EMISSION & CLAIM-TO-EVIDENCE BINDING

PURPOSE

Implement claim-level provenance on top of the durable evidence foundation proven in Sprint 29K.2.

We are NOT building the Evidence UI in this sprint.

The objective is to prove that when a specialist produces Completed Work, important findings and recommendations can be represented as structured claims and bound to the evidence actually available during that execution.

The human-readable Completed Work remains the primary deliverable.

Claim metadata is provenance metadata. It must not replace, rewrite, summarise, or degrade the report.

==================================================
NON-NEGOTIABLE PRINCIPLES
==================================================

1. Do not run a second LLM pass over Completed Work to reconstruct claims.

2. Do not perform a second KRS retrieval for claim binding.

3. Claims must be emitted during the same specialist generation that produces the work.

4. Claim evidence may reference ONLY EvidenceChunks present in the EvidencePack used for that execution.

5. Never treat passageSnapshot (first 800 chars) as automatically being the supporting passage.

6. Direct observations require positive evidence.

7. Absence findings require an explicit absence-search record, not merely "the model did not see anything".

8. Inferences must be distinguishable from statements directly contained in source material.

9. Recommendations must link to the finding(s) that justify them.

10. External requirements must NOT be represented as authoritative unless supported by an approved external evidence source.

11. No chain-of-thought is to be stored.

12. Existing Completed Work prose, quality review, approval, export and evidence persistence behaviour must remain intact.

13. Evidence provenance failure must not silently become fabricated provenance.

==================================================
PART A — CLAIM TAXONOMY
==================================================

Implement canonical:

type ClaimType =
  | "observation"
  | "absence_finding"
  | "inference"
  | "external_requirement"
  | "recommendation";

Definitions:

observation
= something directly supported by source evidence.

Example:
"Complaints must be acknowledged within three business days."

absence_finding
= a relevant requirement, control, responsibility, timeframe, process or other element was searched for but not found within the defined evidence scope.

Example:
"The policy does not define escalation ownership."

inference
= professional analysis derived from one or more supported observations/absence findings but not directly stated by the source.

Example:
"Unclear escalation ownership may result in inconsistent complaint handling."

external_requirement
= statement about legislation, regulation, standard, regulator guidance or other external authority.

This type MUST fail validation unless a supporting evidence link comes from an explicitly recognised external/legislative source.

Model training knowledge is NOT evidence.

recommendation
= proposed action derived from one or more findings.

Example:
"Define a named escalation owner and document their authority."

==================================================
PART D — SUPPORTING SPAN
==================================================

This is important.

EvidencePack contains full chunk text.

The specialist may identify:

supportingSpan

for a claim.

But the server MUST verify that supportingSpan exists verbatim within the referenced EvidenceChunk.text.

Do not trust the LLM blindly.

If:

chunkId exists in EvidencePack
AND
supportingSpan is an exact substring of EvidenceChunk.text

then persist the binding.

If the span does not exist:

do not fabricate or fuzzy-rewrite the quotation.

Mark/reject that evidence binding.

Do not fail the entire Completed Work because one claim binding is invalid.

Record the provenance validation failure.

Do NOT replace the supportingSpan with passageSnapshot.

==================================================
PART E — CLAIM EVIDENCE VALIDATION
==================================================

Before persistence validate every claim.

OBSERVATION

Requires:
at least one valid direct_support evidence link.

The referenced chunkId MUST exist in the execution EvidencePack.

If not:
claim provenance status = unsupported.

Do not present it internally as grounded.

--------------------------------------------------

INFERENCE

Requires:
at least one relatedClaimId pointing to:

observation
or
absence_finding
or another supported inference where recursion is safely bounded.

Evidence links may additionally provide context.

Inference must not masquerade as direct source wording.

--------------------------------------------------

RECOMMENDATION

Requires:
at least one relatedClaimId pointing to a finding.

A recommendation may have supporting evidence but must primarily derive from findings.

Example:

F1 = absence_finding
"No escalation owner is defined."

R1 = recommendation
"Define a named escalation owner."

R1.relatedClaimIds = ["F1"]

--------------------------------------------------

EXTERNAL_REQUIREMENT

Requires:

relationship = external_authority

AND evidence source type / authority classification demonstrates that the source is an approved external authority.

Until an approved external evidence provider/source exists:

external_requirement claims without such evidence MUST be marked unsupported/rejected.

Training knowledge does not satisfy this requirement.

==================================================
PART F — CONTRADICTIONS
==================================================

Support structured contradiction findings.

Example:

Claim:

"The policy contains conflicting acknowledgement timeframes."

Evidence:

chunk A:
"within three (3) business days"

chunk B:
"within five (5) business days"

Both evidence links must be persisted.

Use relationship:

contradiction

for both relevant evidence links.

Both supportingSpan values must independently pass exact-substring validation.

Do not attempt to decide which statement is authoritative unless evidence supports that conclusion.

==================================================
PART G — ABSENCE FINDINGS
==================================================

This is the highest-integrity part of the sprint.

Do NOT allow an absence_finding merely because the specialist says:

"I could not find X."

An absence claim requires structured retrieval evidence.

Minimum AbsenceEvidenceRecord:

{
  "searchTerms": [],
  "sourceScope": [],
  "retrievalFilters": {
    "specialistCode": "...",
    "scopeMode": "...",
    "minConfidenceThreshold": 0
  },
  "sectionsExamined": [],
  "totalCandidatesRetrieved": 0,
  "passedThresholdCount": 0,
  "topRelevanceScores": [],
  "matchingRequirementFound": false,
  "confidenceOfAbsence": 0
}

IMPORTANT:

Investigate the existing KRS architecture before implementing this.

If the current single retrieval operation cannot truthfully provide claim-specific absence-search evidence, DO NOT invent it.

In that case:

1. implement the claim schema,
2. allow the specialist to propose an absence claim,
3. classify it as absence_unverified / unsupported provenance,
4. document exactly what KRS extension would be required in Sprint 29K.4.

Do NOT perform hidden additional retrievals in this sprint merely to make the absence claim appear proven.

We need truth, not a green test.

==================================================
PART H — PROVENANCE STATUS
==================================================

Add an explicit provenance state to claims if required by the architecture.

Recommended:

type ClaimProvenanceStatus =
  | "grounded"
  | "unsupported"
  | "unverified_absence"
  | "invalid_binding";

This prevents an emitted claim from automatically being treated as proven merely because a row exists.

Persist the status.

A claim row is NOT equivalent to a grounded claim.

Completed Work itself must survive provenance failure.

Expected:
