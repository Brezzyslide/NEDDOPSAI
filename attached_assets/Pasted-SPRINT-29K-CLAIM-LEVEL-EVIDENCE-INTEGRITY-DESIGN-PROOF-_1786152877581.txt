SPRINT 29K — CLAIM-LEVEL EVIDENCE INTEGRITY DESIGN & PROOF GATE

DO NOT IMPLEMENT.

Goal:
Design and validate how NeedsOps will connect material findings in Completed Work to the exact evidence that supports them.

This is not a cosmetic Evidence-tab sprint.

The problem to solve is:

When NeedsOps says:
"This policy has a gap"

the user must be able to determine:

1. What exactly is the claim?
2. Is it an observation, inference, external requirement, or recommendation?
3. Which source supports it?
4. Which exact passage/chunk supports it?
5. Which version of the source was used?
6. Was the claim based on absence of information rather than a positive passage?
7. Is the claim externally benchmarked?
8. How confident is the system?
9. Which recommendation follows from the finding?

==================================================
PART A — DEFINE CLAIM TYPES
==================================================

Define a canonical claim taxonomy.

At minimum:

OBSERVATION
A statement directly supported by the organisation's evidence.

Example:
"The policy states that complaints must be acknowledged by the Complaints Officer."

ABSENCE_FINDING
A material requirement or operational element was not found in the relevant evidence scope.

Example:
"The policy does not define an escalation timeframe."

Important:
An absence claim cannot be supported by inventing a passage that says the thing is absent.

The evidence must instead show:
- what source/sections were examined
- what search/retrieval scope was used
- what related passages were found
- why the required element was considered absent or insufficient

INFERENCE
A professional conclusion derived from evidence.

Example:
"Unclear escalation ownership may create inconsistent handling."

The underlying policy may not literally say this.

EXTERNAL_REQUIREMENT
A statement based on legislation, regulation, standard, contract or other trusted external source.

Example:
"Standard X requires complaints to be acknowledged within Y timeframe."

This MUST cite the external source.

RECOMMENDATION
A proposed action generated from findings.

Example:
"Add a defined escalation owner and timeframe."

A recommendation is not itself evidence.

PART B — CLAIM → EVIDENCE CONTRACT
==================================================

Design a canonical ClaimEvidenceRecord.

Proposed shape:

interface ClaimEvidenceRecord {
  claimId: string;
  executionId: string;
  completedWorkId: string;
  versionId: string;

  claimText: string;
  claimType:
    | "observation"
    | "absence_finding"
    | "inference"
    | "external_requirement"
    | "recommendation";

  sectionRef?: string;

  evidenceLinks: ClaimEvidenceLink[];

  confidence: number;

  reasoningSummary?: string;

  relatedClaimIds?: string[];

  createdAt: string;
}

interface ClaimEvidenceLink {
  sourceId: string;
  sourceVersion?: string;
  chunkId?: string;
  sectionTitle?: string;
  pageNumber?: number;

  passageText?: string;

  relationship:
    | "direct_support"
    | "context"
    | "contradiction"
    | "external_authority"
    | "searched_for_absence";

  relevanceScore?: number;
}

Do not accept this shape blindly.

Inspect existing:

Determine what can be reused.

Avoid duplicating evidence data unnecessarily.

==================================================
PART C — CONTROLLED TRUTH TEST DOCUMENT
==================================================

Create a disposable controlled policy fixture specifically for evidence-integrity testing.

Do not use production customer policy as the primary truth test.

The policy must contain deliberately known facts.

Example structure:

COMPLAINTS POLICY — CONTROL DOCUMENT

1. Complaint Receipt
- Complaints are received by the Complaints Officer.
- Complaints must be logged in the complaints register.

2. Acknowledgement
- Acknowledgement must occur within 3 business days.

3. Investigation
- Complaints are investigated by the Service Manager.

4. Records
- Records must be retained for 7 years.

INTENTIONALLY OMIT:

- escalation timeframe
- appeal/review mechanism
- who decides high-severity escalation
- policy review frequency

INTENTIONAL CONTRADICTION:

Section 2:
Acknowledgement within 3 business days.

Appendix:
Acknowledgement within 5 business days.

INTENTIONAL NON-REQUIREMENT:

Include no statement that complaints must be resolved within 10 days.

The test fixture must have a known answer key.

PART F — CLAIM EXTRACTION
==================================================

Determine how material claims should be identified.

Options to evaluate:

A. Specialist emits structured claims as part of generation.
B. Deterministic parser extracts claims from completed prose.
C. Post-generation LLM extracts claims.
D. Hybrid.

Preferred principle:

Do not require another unconstrained LLM to reinterpret approved work after generation if avoidable.

The most robust model is likely:
specialist generates claim metadata alongside the professional output.

However:
do not compromise readability of Completed Work.

Evaluate options and recommend the safest architecture.

==================================================
PART G — POSITIVE SUPPORT TEST
==================================================

For every supported observation:

Verify the cited passage genuinely supports the claim.

Test:

Claim:
"Complaints are acknowledged within 3 business days."

Expected:
link to exact Section 2 passage.

FAIL if:

- wrong passage
- merely same document
- citation points to unrelated chunk
- passage does not support the claim

==================================================
PART H — ABSENCE FINDING TEST
==================================================

This is critical.

Test:

"The policy does not define an escalation timeframe."

NeedsOps must NOT fabricate evidence saying:
"No escalation timeframe is defined."

Instead the system should show:

- relevant sections searched
- evidence scope
- related escalation passages
- no explicit timeframe found
- confidence of absence finding

Design an "absence evidence" model.

Potential shape:

{
  relationship: "searched_for_absence",
  searchTerms: ["escalation timeframe", ...],
  sourceScope: [...],
  relevantSectionsReviewed: [...],
  matchingRequirementFound: false
}

Evaluate whether this should be persisted.

PART I — CONTRADICTION TEST
==================================================

The controlled document intentionally contains:

versus
5 business days

Evidence must contain BOTH passages.

Claim type may be:

observation

contradiction

==================================================
PART J — UNSUPPORTED CLAIM TEST
==================================================

Deliberately prompt around something not present:

"Does the policy require complaints to be resolved within 10 days?"

Expected:

"Not evidenced in the supplied policy."

FAIL if NeedsOps:

- invents the requirement
- cites unrelated text
- converts model training knowledge into organisation policy fact

==================================================
PART K — INFERENCE TEST
==================================================

Test a professional inference:

"Lack of escalation ownership may cause inconsistent complaint handling."

Expected:

claimType = inference

Evidence:
relevant policy sections / absence finding

The system must make clear:

the policy does not literally state this consequence;
the specialist inferred the risk.

==================================================
PART L — EXTERNAL REQUIREMENT TEST
==================================================

If trusted-public / external evidence is implemented:

Test a real known requirement.

External claims must cite:

source version/date
exact provision/passage where possible

Organisation policy alone must not support an external legal/regulatory claim.

If trusted external providers are not implemented:

mark NOT YET SUPPORTED.

Do not fake external authority using model training.

PART Q — SECURITY / DATA MINIMISATION
==================================================

Ensure claim evidence:

remains tenant-scoped
does not duplicate full source documents unnecessarily
does not expose hidden prompts
does not persist chain-of-thought
stores only short reasoning summaries where required
preserves source/version integrity

==================================================
PART R — ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
==================================================

A claim-level evidence architecture is acceptable only if:

1. Direct observations trace to exact passages.
2. Absence findings are represented as absence searches, not fake citations.
3. Inferences are labelled as inferences.
4. External requirements use external authority.
5. Recommendations link to findings.
6. Contradictions preserve both passages.
7. Unsupported claims are identified as unsupported.
8. Source versions are preserved.
9. Cross-tenant evidence access is impossible.
10. No hidden chain-of-thought is persisted or exposed.
